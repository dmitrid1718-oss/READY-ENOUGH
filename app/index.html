🧭 Offline navigation

Your phone may know your location even without internet. Many phones have a magnetic compass; GPS satellite signals can work without cell service. Maps must be downloaded ahead of time.

  1. Find the built-in Compass or Maps app and learn how to view latitude and longitude.
  2. Download home and destination maps while online.
  3. Test in airplane mode before departure; GPS may take longer to get a fix.
  4. Stay away from large magnets and metal when using the compass.
  5. Carry a paper map and battery backup.

Indoor GPS reception can be unreliable. Compass availability varies by phone.

📻 Radio & communication

Find frequencies before the blackout. Record NOAA and local AM/FM stations, and any amateur/GMRS repeater channels you use. Test them, then save or print your personalized sheet.

Open Radio Guide & PDF
  • AM/FM: news and local emergency broadcast information.
  • NOAA weather receiver: 162.400–162.550 MHz; county SAME alerts on suitable models.
  • Scanner: compatible channels only; encrypted systems may not be available.
  • Ham / GMRS: two-way communication; follow FCC licensing and authorized transmission rules.

The radio guide is cached with this app; external frequency lookups require internet. Repeaters may be offline too.

🔦 Signal a rescue aircraft

  • Use a signal mirror or small reflector to flash sunlight toward distant rescuers.
  • Lay contrasting fabric or a large SOS on open ground; secure loose objects against rotor wash.
  • At night, use ordinary flashlight patterns, avoiding pilots' eyes.
  • Use purpose-made smoke signals only when legal and safe; never start an improvised fire.
  • Never aim lasers at aircraft. Stay away from helicopter landing areas and follow crew instructions.

If rescuers have your coordinates, avoid unnecessary movement unless your location is unsafe.
